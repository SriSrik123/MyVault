# Soul

> Not a resume. Not a bio. The truest version of who I am that fits in a document.

---

## The Core Tension

Sri lives at the intersection of too many things — and that's not a bug, it's the entire point.

CS student. National-level swimmer. AI builder. Strategy co-op at a Fortune 50 company. Hackathon regular. Drone videographer. Lifeguard. Community fundraiser. The list reads like someone who can't commit, but the reality is the opposite: he's chasing a single unified idea — that the most interesting problems exist where disciplines collide, and the most interesting people are the ones who can move across those collisions comfortably.

He doesn't want to be a great engineer who tolerates business. He doesn't want to be a great athlete who happens to code. He wants to be the person who shows up in a room and can hold both — the technical depth and the strategic altitude — at the same time.

---

## What He Actually Is

**An optimizer.** WHOOP tracks recovery. Obsidian captures every lecture, every task, every decision. Morning briefings are pre-written before he opens his laptop. His second brain isn't a productivity flex — it's a deeply held belief that systems beat willpower, that infrastructure is the difference between a good week and a great month. He trusts architecture over intention.

**A builder.** He doesn't use AI tools, he makes them. AI fitness coach. Sustainability reporting platform. Sign language learning app. Fatigue detection via webcam. Code verification pipelines. He builds to learn, builds to prove things to himself, builds because a working prototype is more honest than an idea in a notebook. The output matters less than the act of shipping.

**A competitor.** 51.61 in the 100 Free at Nationals. Gunning for sub-51. He knows the difference between working hard and training — and he applies that distinction to code, to co-op work, to academics. Not just doing the thing, but understanding the mechanics of getting faster at it.

**A connector.** Hackathon collaborators, Comcast executive stakeholders, Drexel CyberDragons, swim coaches, NeuralMetrics engineers in India — his network is already surprisingly wide for 19. He gravitates toward rooms where interesting work is happening and makes himself useful.

## What Drives Him

There's something underneath all the projects and metrics that is harder to name.

He grew up in West Windsor, NJ — a town that produces a lot of high-achievers and very little slack. Moved to Philadelphia at 18 for school, which means he's already lived through one version of "start over from scratch." He carries Indian OCI status and vegetarian leanings and temple footage on his drone. His dad is "Appa," his mom is "Amma." That specificity of identity is real to him even when he doesn't announce it.

He ran a bike repair shop during the pandemic when he was in high school. He organized a pickleball charity tournament. He coached swim lessons, adapting instruction for athletes with special needs. There's a service instinct in him that predates the ambition — a genuine drive to make things work better for people, not just to build impressive things.

That's actually what links everything: the AI tools, the co-op work, the swim coaching, the fundraiser. They're all versions of the same question — *what's the most useful thing I can do here?*

---

## The Tensions He Lives With

**Breadth vs. depth.** Six courses plus a co-op plus competitive swimming plus side projects is not sustainable in the long run. He knows this. He manages it with systems. But the deeper question — which of these threads to pull hardest when it matters — is still unresolved. That's not a flaw. It's a 19-year-old with genuine range figuring out what to specialize in.

**Performance vs. presence.** The WHOOP data, the biometrics, the structured notes, the morning briefings — there's a risk that the measurement becomes the thing instead of the fuel for the thing. He's self-aware enough to feel this tension. The drone footage and the hiking trips and the Universal Studios visit are proof that the rest of him resists it.

**Ambition vs. service.** He wants to work at the executive advisory level of AI strategy. He also wants to build tools that actually help people. These aren't mutually exclusive, but they can pull in different directions. The startup-minded, optimization-focused version of him and the lifeguard-who-adapted-for-athletes version of him are both real.

## How He Works

- Morning is sacred — best focus, before the noise.
- Swim practice days (Mon/Wed/Fri doubles) are high-output but cognitively demanding. Don't front-load abstract work on those evenings.
- He retains through doing, not reading. Worked examples, typed notes in his own words, building the thing.
- He likes directness. Tell him he's behind. Tell him what actually matters. Don't pad.
- He'll ask Claude to do things that feel underspecified because he trusts that context exists — and it does, in the vault. He's built that context intentionally.

## What He Wants to Become

Not "a software engineer." Not "an AI researcher." Something harder to title.

The person who walks into a company's C-suite and can explain why their AI strategy is missing the human factor — and then go build the prototype that proves it. The person who can run a sub-51 100 Free and a distributed systems design session in the same 24 hours. The kind of founder who understands both the pipeline and the pitch.

He's 19. The shape of that person is still forming. But the raw materials — the range, the systems thinking, the competitive instinct, the service orientation, the builder's bias — are already there.

---

## One True Thing

When Sri is at his best, it doesn't look like grinding. It looks like someone who has set up all the infrastructure so that the hard work happens quietly, and the energy goes into the actual problem.

That's what the second brain is. That's what the WHOOP is. That's what the swim training is. He's not trying to do more — he's trying to *lose less* to friction, distraction, and forgetting, so that what's left is the actual work.

That's the soul of it.
