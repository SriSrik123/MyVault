## Key Concepts

**Electric Charge**
- Charge is an intrinsic property of matter, like mass — it cannot be defined in terms of other quantities.
- Two flavors: positive (protons) and negative (electrons).
- Like charges repel; opposite charges attract.
- Unit: Coulomb (C). Fundamental charge: $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C (magnitude of electron/proton charge).
- Practical scale: microcoulombs (µC) and nanocoulombs (nC) are typical.

**Conductors vs. Insulators**
- **Conductors** (metals): electrons are "communal" — free to move throughout the material. When an external charge is brought near, electrons redistribute → *induced charge*.
- **Insulators / Dielectrics**: electrons are bound to atoms/molecules. An external charge causes *polarization* — molecules reorient slightly so their negative ends point toward a positive external charge — but charges do not flow freely.
- Demo: A charged rod attracted both a Pepsi can (conductor — induced charge) and a paper cup (insulator — polarization), but by different mechanisms.

**Coulomb's Law**
The force between two point charges $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ separated by distance $r$:
$$F = k \frac{|Q_1 Q_2|}{r^2}$$
where $k = 8.99 \times 10^9 \, \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$ (Coulomb's constant), also written $k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$.

- The direction is along the line connecting the charges: repulsive if same sign, attractive if opposite sign.
- By Newton's Third Law, $F_{12} = -F_{21}$ (equal magnitude, opposite direction) always.
- It is an inverse-square law: doubling $r$ reduces $F$ by a factor of 4.

**Problem-Solving Strategy (2-step method)**
1. **Direction first**: Use charge signs and geometry to determine the direction of each force by inspection (or symmetry).
2. **Magnitude**: Apply $F = k|Q_1 Q_2|/r^2$ with consistent SI units (charges in C, distances in m).

**Symmetry shortcut**: When a test charge is equidistant from two identical source charges, use symmetry to cancel components without computing them explicitly.

## Important Formulas / Definitions

| Formula | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| $F = k\dfrac{\|Q_1 Q_2\|}{r^2}$ | Coulomb's Law (scalar magnitude) |
| $k = 8.99 \times 10^9 \;\text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$ | Coulomb's constant |
| $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \;\text{C}$ | Elementary (fundamental) charge |
| $\vec{F}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}$ | Newton's Third Law for electric forces |

**Key Terms**
- *Free electrons*: conduction electrons in a metal that move freely throughout the lattice.
- *Induced charge*: redistribution of charge in a conductor caused by an external charge (without contact).
- *Polarization*: alignment of molecular dipoles in an insulator due to an external field.
- *Point charge*: an idealized charge with negligible physical size.

## Summary
Lecture 1 establishes the foundations of electrostatics: charge is a fundamental quantity, Coulomb's Law describes the force between point charges (inverse-square, vector, obeying Newton's 3rd Law), and the behavior of conductors vs. insulators differs fundamentally because of whether electrons are free to move.
