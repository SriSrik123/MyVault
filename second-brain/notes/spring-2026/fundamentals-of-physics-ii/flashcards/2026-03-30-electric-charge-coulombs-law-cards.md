# Flashcards — Electric Charge, Conductors, and Coulomb's Law — PHYS-102
**Date:** 2026-03-30

---
**Q:** State Coulomb's Law and define all variables.
**A:** $F = k\frac{|Q_1||Q_2|}{r^2}$, where $k = 9\times10^9\,\text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2$ is Coulomb's constant, $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ are the magnitudes of the two point charges in Coulombs, and $r$ is the distance between them in meters. The force is repulsive if the charges have the same sign and attractive if they have opposite signs.

---
**Q:** What is Coulomb's constant $k$ in terms of the permittivity of free space $\epsilon_0$?
**A:** $k = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$, where $\epsilon_0 = 8.85\times10^{-12}\,\text{C}^2/\text{N·m}^2$.

---
**Q:** How does Coulomb's force change if the separation between two charges is doubled?
**A:** Because Coulomb's law is an inverse-square law ($F \propto 1/r^2$), doubling the separation reduces the force by a factor of 4.

---
**Q:** What does Newton's Third Law say about the forces between two charges?
**A:** The force that charge 1 exerts on charge 2 is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force that charge 2 exerts on charge 1: $\vec{F}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}$.

---
**Q:** What is the key difference between a conductor and an insulator at the atomic level?
**A:** In a conductor (e.g., a metal), outer electrons are not bound to individual atoms — they are "free" and belong to the entire lattice, allowing macroscopic charge flow. In an insulator, electrons are tightly bound to atoms or ions and cannot migrate freely.

---
**Q:** What is a dielectric and how does it differ from an ordinary insulator?
**A:** A dielectric is a special insulator whose constituent molecules act as electric dipoles. Under an external electric field, the dipoles can **reorient** (rotate) to partially align with the field, even though no charges physically migrate through the material. An ordinary insulator does not exhibit this significant reorientation.

---
**Q:** What is induced charge, and in which type of material does it occur?
**A:** Induced charge is the redistribution of free charges in a **conductor** caused by an external charged object. The external charge attracts opposite charges and repels like charges toward the far end, producing a net charge on each surface. When the external charge is removed, the charges redistribute uniformly again.

---
**Q:** What is an electric dipole?
**A:** An electric dipole is a system of two equal and opposite charges ($+Q$ and $-Q$) separated by a small distance. It is characterized by its dipole moment. Dipoles are important in dielectrics because individual molecules can behave as dipoles that reorient under an external field.

---
**Q:** What is the two-step problem-solving strategy for Coulomb's Law problems?
**A:** Step 1 — **Direction**: Use the sign of the charges and symmetry to determine the direction of the net force by inspection. Step 2 — **Magnitude**: Plug the magnitudes of the charges (in Coulombs) and the distance (in meters) into $F = k|Q_1||Q_2|/r^2$. Do not insert signs into the formula; direction was handled in Step 1.

---
**Q:** How does symmetry simplify multi-charge Coulomb's Law problems?
**A:** If a test charge is placed symmetrically between two equal charges, the force components along one axis cancel and components along the other axis add. This allows you to determine the direction and simplify the magnitude calculation without explicitly writing out all vector components.

---
**Q:** What unit must charges be in when using $F = kQ_1Q_2/r^2$, and what unit must distances be in?
**A:** Charges must be in **Coulombs (C)** and distances must be in **meters (m)**. Convert micro-Coulombs ($\mu$C) to Coulombs and centimeters to meters before substituting.

---
**Q:** What happens to the polarization (molecular reorientation) in a dielectric when the external charge is removed?
**A:** The dipole molecules return to their random orientations; there is no permanent polarization (unlike in a ferroelectric material). The net effect disappears because the molecules are not locked into alignment.

---
**Q:** State the superposition principle for electric forces.
**A:** The net force on a charge due to multiple other charges equals the **vector sum** of the individual Coulomb forces from each charge, calculated as if only those two charges existed.

---
**Q:** Why is writing things by hand emphasized in this course?
**A:** Writing by hand forces active engagement with the material. You may think you understand something from reading or watching, but the act of writing it out reveals gaps in understanding. Passive exposure (e.g., just looking at a screen) is insufficient for mastering physics problem-solving.

---
**Q:** What is the formal vector form of Coulomb's Law?
**A:** $\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Q_1 Q_2}{r_{12}^2}\hat{r}_{12}$, where $\hat{r}_{12}$ is the unit vector pointing from charge 1 to charge 2 and $r_{12}$ is the distance between them.
