# Electric Field & Coulomb's Law — PHYS-102
**Date:** 2026-04-01
**Source:** Phys102 day2_original

## Overview
This lecture revisited the concept of induced charge, developed Coulomb's Law as the governing equation for forces between charges, and introduced the electric field as an alternative framework that avoids the "action at a distance" problem. The field model—where a charge creates a field throughout space and a second charge interacts with that field—is central to all of electrostatics.

## Key Concepts

### Induced Charge vs. Deposited Charge
- When a charged object touches a conductor, electrons redistribute and positive charge is deposited.
- Induced charge occurs without direct contact (via induction), whereas deposited charge requires conduction.

### Coulomb's Law
The force between two point charges Q₁ and Q₂ separated by distance r:

$$F = k \frac{Q_1 Q_2}{r^2}$$

- k ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C² (Coulomb's constant; also written as $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$)
- This is an **inverse square law**: doubling the distance reduces force by a factor of 4.
- Force is attractive for opposite charges, repulsive for like charges.

### Action at a Distance (and Why We Need Fields)
Coulomb's Law raises a conceptual problem: *how does Q₂ "know" Q₁ is there?* Newton faced the same issue with gravity. The resolution is the **field** model:
- Q₁ creates an **electric field** that fills all surrounding space.
- When Q₂ enters that region it interacts with the local field, not directly with Q₁.

### Electric Field
The electric field **E** at a point in space due to a charge Q:

$$E = k\frac{Q}{r^2}$$

- Units: N/C (newtons per coulomb)
- Direction: away from a positive source charge, toward a negative source charge.
- The force on a test charge q placed in the field: **F** = q**E**

### Field Lines
- Field lines show the **direction** of the electric field at every point.
- Density of lines represents field **strength**.
- For a positive point charge, lines radiate outward in all directions.
- For a negative point charge, lines point inward.
- Convention: field lines are drawn using a small positive **test charge**.

## Important Formulas / Definitions

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| $F = k\frac{Q_1 Q_2}{r^2}$ | Coulomb's Law (force between charges) |
| $E = k\frac{Q}{r^2}$ | Electric field from a point charge |
| $k = 9 \times 10^9$ N·m²/C² | Coulomb's constant |
| $\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}$ C²/N·m² | Permittivity of free space |
| F = qE | Force on test charge q in field E |

**Inverse square law:** if distance doubles → force (or field) drops by a factor of 4.

## Summary
Coulomb's Law gives us the electrostatic force between two charges and has the same mathematical form as Newton's gravitational law. To resolve the conceptual issue of how charges interact across empty space, physicists introduced the electric field: a charge creates a field permeating all of space, and any other charge placed in that field experiences a force. Field lines are a visual tool to represent both the direction and relative magnitude of the field.
