## Formatting rules (points deducted per violation)
- All straight lines (matrix brackets, conclusion bars, dividing lines) must be ruler-drawn or software-straight — visibly straight.
- No dot for multiplication — use parentheses/juxtaposition. No decimals unless the problem requires it.
- Equalities must line up on the left, ~5mm gap from the expressions they relate to.
- No blank pages, no incomplete/unproved work (incomplete work costs **double** the problem's point value), no extraneous marks ("PTO," "continued," etc.).
- Fill in the top-sheet with per-part scores, row totals, and the grand total added correctly.
- Match the Solved Exams' format/organization exactly, but don't blindly copy their answers — they may contain deliberate errors.
- Use a mechanical pencil, 2B/3B/4B lead (not HB), ≥0.7mm diameter, so scans are legible.

## Exam-specific notes ("Particularities" emails)
- **1A** — sets, predicates, basic set-builder problems. All a's+b's correct ≈ B, only 9 pts short of an A per his estimate.
- **1B** — relations, equivalence relations, partial orders, matrices (matrix brackets must be ruler-straight).
- **1C** — counting, direct proof + induction; recursive definitions need correct case count and quantification. "Everyone should get 41" if done properly — good exam to hit the cutoff on.
- **2A** — recursion/induction proofs; must follow the Notes' induction template exactly.
- **2B** — propositional calculus (truth tables, DNF/CNF, natural deduction). Typical score 45–51/exam.
- **2C** — Boolean algebra version of the same material (closest to ordinary high-school algebra, no exponents/multiples). Also typically 45–51/exam, considered the easiest.
- Problems are labeled a (easy) / b (medium) / c (hard) across three questions per exam.
