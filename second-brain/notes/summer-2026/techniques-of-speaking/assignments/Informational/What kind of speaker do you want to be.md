# What Kind of Speaker Do You Want to Be?

**Course:** Techniques of Speaking — Summer 2026
**Assignment:** Personal Speaker Identity Reflection

---

## The Speaker I Want to Be

- I want to be a **confident and persuasive** speaker — someone who walks into a room and immediately commands attention, not through volume or aggression, but through presence and conviction
- My goal is to leave every audience with a changed perspective, a new idea, or a clear call to action — not just information

---

## Why Confidence Matters to Me

- Confidence is the foundation of persuasion — if I don't believe what I'm saying, no one else will either
- I've noticed that the speakers who move me most aren't the smartest in the room — they're the ones who deliver their message without hesitation or apology
- I want to reach the point where I can speak under pressure (debates, presentations, high-stakes pitches) and stay composed and clear

---

## What Persuasion Means to Me

- Persuasion isn't manipulation — it's the ability to connect my reasoning to what the audience already values
- A persuasive speaker listens as much as they talk — understanding your audience is what makes your argument land
- I want to be able to adapt my argument in real time based on how the room is responding, not just deliver a rehearsed script

---

## Strengths I'm Building On

- I already think logically and can structure an argument well — I want to bring that same clarity into my delivery
- I'm comfortable with data and evidence, and I want to use that to build credibility before asking anyone to be persuaded
- I tend to speak directly and concisely — I see that as an asset in persuasion, where every word should earn its place

---

## Areas I'm Working to Improve

- **Filler words** — cutting "um," "like," and "you know" to project more authority
- **Eye contact** — holding it longer to build trust and show I believe what I'm saying
- **Pacing** — learning to pause strategically instead of rushing through key points
- **Emotional connection** — balancing logic with enough personal voice that the argument feels human, not robotic

---

## The Bigger Picture

- The kind of speaker I want to be isn't just for class — I want these skills in job interviews, team leadership, and any moment where I need to advocate for myself or an idea I believe in
- Ultimately, I want people to walk away from hearing me speak thinking *"he had a point"* — and then acting on it
