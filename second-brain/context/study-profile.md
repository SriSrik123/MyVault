## How I Learn
- **Preferred format:** Structured notes with clear headers, tables, and worked examples
- **Retention style:** Writing things out by hand or typing them in my own words — passive reading alone doesn't stick
- **Strong at:** Pattern recognition, connecting concepts across subjects, building intuition through worked examples
- **Needs extra effort:** Memorisation without context, abstract proofs without examples, staying ahead when assignments pile up

## Note-Taking Approach
- Lecture recordings → transcribed and saved as structured `.md` notes in the second brain (via recording-to-obsidian skill)
- Notes follow the format: Overview → Key Concepts → Formulas/Definitions → Summary
- Flashcards are auto-generated alongside lecture notes for active recall
- All notes organised by course in `~/Documents/MyVault/second-brain/notes/`

## Study Schedule Preferences
- Best focus: **early morning** and **late evening** (after swim practice)
- Avoid scheduling heavy cognitive work immediately post-double-practice days (Mon/Wed/Fri)
- Study blocks work best at 45–90 min with a short break
- Prefer to batch similar-subject work (e.g., do all math/logic in one sitting)

## Assignment Hygiene
- SE-201 assignments are sequential — missing one cascades; never skip
- CS-265 labs built on activities; submit via `submit` on tux server
- PHYS-102 has two weekly deadlines: Friday (conceptual) + Monday (ACHIEVE numerical)
- CS-270 has in-class labs every session — always attend

## What Works for Me
- Having a morning briefing ready before I open my laptop
- Clear prioritised task list (not a dump of everything, just what matters today)
- Concrete next actions, not vague reminders
- Being told when I'm falling behind before it becomes a crisis

## What Doesn't Work
- Re-explaining context every session
- Vague study advice without specifics to my courses
- Suggestions that ignore my swim practice schedule
